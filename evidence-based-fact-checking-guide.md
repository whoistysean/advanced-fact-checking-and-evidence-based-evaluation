Evidence-Based Fact-Checking and Evaluation Guide

1. Identify the Claim

* What exactly is the AI claiming?
* Does it contain an exact statistic?
* Does it use absolute language such as “always,” “never,” “everyone,” or “guarantees”?
* Is it claiming association or causation?

2. Evaluate Source Credibility

Consider:

* Author expertise and qualifications.
* Publication reputation.
* Research methodology.
* Sample size and representativeness.
* Publication date and relevance.
* References and transparency.
* Independent confirmation from other reliable sources.

3. Assess Evidence Quality

Ask:

* Does the evidence directly support the claim?
* Are the findings consistent?
* Are limitations acknowledged?
* Does the research establish causation or only association?
* Does the evidence support the exact statistic being presented?

4. Classify the Claim

Classification	Meaning
Verified	Reliable evidence supports the claim.
Misleading	The wording exaggerates, distorts, or misrepresents the evidence.
False	Reliable evidence contradicts the claim.
Unverified	Available evidence is insufficient to confirm the claim.

Important: A claim may contain more than one issue. For example, an exact statistic may be unverified while the causal wording is misleading.

5. Write an Evidence-Based Correction

A good correction should:

* Address the original claim directly.
* Explain what the evidence actually supports.
* Avoid unsupported assumptions.
* Acknowledge uncertainty and research limitations.
* Use clear, neutral language.

6. Final Quality Checklist

Before submitting an evaluation, confirm:

* [ ]	I identified the exact claim.
* [ ]	I assessed source credibility.
* [ ]	I examined evidence quality.
* [ ]	I checked whether the evidence supports the exact conclusion.
* [ ]	I distinguished association from causation.
* [ ]	I selected an appropriate classification.
* [ ]	I wrote a clear and balanced correction.

Conclusion

Reliable fact-checking requires more than finding a source that agrees with a claim. It requires examining the quality of the evidence, understanding its limitations, and ensuring that conclusions do not go beyond what the research supports.
