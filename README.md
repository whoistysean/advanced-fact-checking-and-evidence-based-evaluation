Advanced Fact-Checking and Evidence-Based Evaluation

Project Overview

This project demonstrates practical skills in advanced fact-checking and AI response evaluation. It focuses on assessing evidence quality, comparing sources, identifying unsupported claims, distinguishing misleading information from unverified claims, and writing evidence-based corrections.

Skills Demonstrated

* Evaluating source credibility and research quality
* Comparing scientific studies and systematic reviews
* Identifying unsupported statistics and exaggerated claims
* Distinguishing association from causation
* Classifying claims as verified, misleading, false, or unverified
* Writing accurate, balanced corrections
* Applying evidence-based reasoning to AI responses

Practical Exercises

1. Evaluating Research Evidence: Compared hypothetical research sources to assess claims about instrumental music and examination performance.
2. Assessing Conflicting Evidence: Evaluated claims about breakfast, concentration, and academic performance.
3. Checking Statistical Claims: Investigated an unsupported claim that caffeine increases productivity by exactly 40%.
4. Distinguishing Misleading from Unverified Claims: Examined an unsupported claim that drinking green tea every morning reduces diabetes risk by 35%.

Evaluation Approach

Each exercise applies the following principles:

* Check whether the source is credible and relevant.
* Examine the research method, sample size, and evidence quality.
* Determine whether the evidence supports the exact claim.
* Identify exaggerations, unsupported statistics, and causal assumptions.
* Write corrections that accurately reflect the available evidence.

Key Learning Outcomes

This project strengthened my ability to evaluate scientific claims critically, recognize limitations in research evidence, avoid confusing correlation with causation, and provide balanced feedback when assessing AI-generated information.

Project Note

The exercises in this repository are educational simulations designed to demonstrate fact-checking and evaluation skills. They are not presented as original scientific research or professional client work.
